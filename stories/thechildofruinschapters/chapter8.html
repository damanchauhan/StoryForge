The Child of Ruin

Chapter 8: One More Night

🏠 StoryForge

Elara woke to the smell of breakfast.

For a few moments she simply lay there.

The bed was warm.

The cabin was quiet.

Somewhere nearby, wood crackled softly in the fireplace.

Then a loud crash echoed from outside.

Followed by:

“Ow!”

A pause.

Then:

“Grandpa! The ground attacked me!”

Elara blinked.

A familiar sigh answered.

“No, Rowan.”

“It did.”

“No.”

“It absolutely did.”

The girl buried her face in the blanket.

Trying very hard not to laugh.

Breakfast was noisy.

Mostly because Rowan existed.

“Where are you from?”

“A village.”

“What village?”

“A village.”

“That’s not a name.”

“It’s enough of a name.”

“No it isn’t.”

Aldric silently pushed a bowl toward Rowan.

The boy immediately stopped talking.

Food had always been one of the few things capable of defeating him.

Unlike Rowan, Elara wasn’t used to sitting around.

After breakfast she helped clean.

Then carried water.

Then gathered herbs.

By midday she had somehow completed more work than Rowan.

The boy found this deeply offensive.

“I help.”

Aldric raised an eyebrow.

“You brought home three rocks yesterday.”

“They were important rocks.”

“No.”

“They looked important.”

The old man sighed.

Elara laughed.

That was all the encouragement Rowan needed.

A few minutes later he grabbed her wrist.

“Come on.”

Before she could object, he dragged her outside.

The mountain quickly became Rowan’s personal tour.

“This is the stream.”

Elara stared.

“It’s a stream.”

Rowan nodded proudly.

“It has water.”

A short while later.

“This is the tree I fell out of.”

Elara looked up.

“It’s not very tall.”

“I fell three times.”

“That doesn’t make it better.”

Ten minutes later.

“This is where I got stuck.”

“In what?”

“Mud.”

“Why?”

“I wanted to know how deep it was.”

Elara stared.

“And?”

“It was deep.”

The answer explained far more about Rowan than she would have liked.

The hours passed quickly.

The mountain felt peaceful.

Simple.

The kind of place where nothing ever happened.

For someone else, it might have felt boring.

For Elara, it felt strangely comforting.

“Race you.”

She blinked.

“What?”

Rowan pointed toward a hill.

“Race.”

“Why?”

He thought about it.

Then shrugged.

“Why not?”

A few moments later they were running.

The wind rushed past them.

Grass bent beneath their feet.

For a brief moment neither thought about anything except reaching the top first.

Elara won.

Barely.

Very barely.

Rowan collapsed beside her.

Breathing heavily.

“You cheated.”

She frowned.

“How?”

“I don’t know yet.”

The answer came immediately.

Elara laughed.

A genuine laugh.

Bright and unrestrained.

The kind that escaped before she could stop it.

Rowan stared.

Elara immediately looked away.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You laughed.”

“No I didn’t.”

“You definitely did.”

The girl picked up a handful of grass.

Rowan wisely decided to stop talking.

For a while they sat quietly.

The afternoon sun painted the mountains gold.

A gentle breeze drifted across the hilltop.

Elara glanced sideways.

Then paused.

…

“What?”

Rowan looked at her.

The girl looked away.

“Nothing.”

A moment later she stood.

“We should go back.”

By the time they returned, dinner was already cooking.

The smell alone was enough to make Rowan sprint the last stretch home.

Elara followed more slowly.

A small smile tugged at her lips.

Dinner was even louder than breakfast.

Rowan asked questions.

Aldric ignored most of them.

Elara answered the ones she felt like answering.

It was strangely normal.

The kind of normal she hadn’t realized she missed.

Night eventually settled over the mountain.

The three sat around the fire.

The flames danced across the wooden walls.

Outside, the forest slept beneath the stars.

For a while nobody spoke.

Then Elara quietly stood.

“I should leave tomorrow.”

The words lingered in the room.

Rowan looked up.

Aldric remained focused on the fire.

Silence stretched between them.

Then the old man shrugged.

“You can stay another day.”

Elara froze.

“What?”

Aldric poked at the fire.

“As long as you’re helping.”

The answer came so casually that it almost sounded accidental.

Rowan smiled.

“Good.”

As though there had never been another possibility.

As though she had already been staying there for years.

Elara looked between them.

The stubborn old man pretending not to care.

The boy who seemed incapable of leaving people alone.

The warm cabin.

The crackling fire.

The mountain beyond the walls.

For the first time since arriving…

The thought of leaving felt wrong.

So for that night at least—

She stayed.
