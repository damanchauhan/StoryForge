The Child of Ruin

Chapter 9: Beyond the Mountain

🏠 StoryForge

The stream rushed gently beside them.

Sunlight danced across the water.

A fishing line hung lazily from Rowan’s hand.

Meanwhile, Elara had somehow managed to catch absolutely nothing.

For the third time.

“This is impossible.”

Rowan looked over.

“No.”

“It is.”

“No.”

Elara narrowed her eyes.

The boy pointed toward the water.

“You’re scaring them.”

“The fish?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“You keep talking.”

The girl stared.

“You’re talking.”

“Not as much.”

Several minutes later, Rowan pulled another fish from the stream.

Elara looked personally offended.

“How?”

The boy shrugged.

“I asked nicely.”

The fish was thrown directly at him.

For a while they sat quietly.

The mountain breeze drifted through the trees.

The stream continued its endless journey downhill.

Eventually Elara spoke.

“This stream is tiny.”

Rowan immediately sat upright.

“Tiny?”

“Yes.”

“It’s huge.”

The girl laughed.

“This isn’t huge.”

“It absolutely is.”

“You’ve never seen a river, have you?”

Rowan blinked.

“What’s a river?”

The conversation stopped.

Elara stared.

Rowan stared back.

The girl slowly lowered her fishing rod.

“What do you mean, what’s a river?”

“I mean what’s a river.”

“It’s…”

She paused.

How exactly did someone explain a river?

“It’s like this stream.”

Rowan nodded.

“Okay.”

“But much bigger.”

“How much bigger?”

“Really big.”

The boy frowned.

“That doesn’t explain anything.”

Several minutes later Elara drew a line in the dirt.

“This is the stream.”

Then she drew a much larger one.

“This is a river.”

Rowan stared.

Then stared some more.

Then pointed.

“You’re lying.”

“I’m not.”

“Nobody needs that much water.”

The argument somehow lasted ten minutes.

As they walked home, the conversation continued.

“There are rivers so wide you can’t see the other side.”

Rowan nearly tripped.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

The boy shook his head.

“Impossible.”

A few moments later Elara pointed toward the sky.

“There are buildings taller than trees.”

“Why?”

“What?”

“Why?”

“Because people build them.”

“Why?”

“I don’t know.”

“Then don’t build them.”

The girl wanted to be annoyed.

Instead she found herself laughing.

Again.

By the time they reached the cabin, Rowan had learned about:

·      Rivers

·      Bridges

·      Markets

·      Cities

And had accepted none of it.

“There are cities with thousands of people.”

Rowan stopped walking.

“Thousands?”

“Yes.”

“Thousands?”

“Yes.”

The boy looked genuinely horrified.

“Why?”

Elara burst out laughing.

That evening they sat outside while Aldric prepared dinner.

The mountain was painted gold by the setting sun.

For a while neither spoke.

Then Rowan pointed toward the horizon.

“What’s over there?”

“More mountains.”

“And after that?”

“Villages.”

“And after that?”

“Cities.”

The boy thought for a moment.

“And after that?”

Elara smiled.

“More cities.”

Rowan looked disappointed.

“That’s repetitive.”

A few minutes later he asked another question.

“What’s a knight?”

Elara immediately pointed toward the cabin.

“Like your grandpa.”

Aldric almost dropped the knife he was using.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

The old man sighed.

The children ignored him.

“Knights protect people.”

Elara continued.

“They fight monsters and bandits.”

Rowan glanced toward Aldric.

Then back at her.

“So Grandpa.”

“No.”

“Definitely Grandpa.”

For a moment Aldric looked strangely uncomfortable.

Then he stood up and walked back inside.

Neither child noticed.

The sun slowly disappeared beyond the mountains.

The world grew quieter.

For the first time all day, Rowan sat silently.

Thinking.

A rare and dangerous occurrence.

Elara noticed.

“What?”

The boy stared toward the distant horizon.

Nothing but mountains.

Forests.

Sky.

The same view he had seen his entire life.

Yet somehow it looked different now.

Bigger.

Further away.

More mysterious.

“Those cities.”

“Hm?”

“Are they really that amazing?”

The question caught Elara off guard.

She thought about it.

Really thought about it.

Then smiled.

“Some are.”

A familiar voice answered from behind them.

“Some aren’t.”

Both turned.

Aldric stood in the doorway.

Arms crossed.

Watching the sunset.

Rowan looked between them.

Confused.

“Have you seen them too?”

The old man was quiet for a moment.

Then:

“Some.”

The answer only made Rowan more curious.

That night, long after dinner ended, Rowan stepped outside.

The mountain was silent.

The stars stretched endlessly above.

He looked toward the horizon.

Toward the world Elara had described.

Rivers.

Cities.

Markets.

Knights.

Thousands of people.

Things he had never seen.

Things he couldn’t quite imagine.

For the first time in his life…

The mountain felt a little smaller.
