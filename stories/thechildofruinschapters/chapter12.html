The Child of Ruin

Chapter 12: The Promise Kept

🏠 StoryForge

The wooden sword finally broke.

Rowan turned the splintered handle over in his hand.

Silent.

Aldric glanced up from the firewood he was splitting.

“Well?”

The boy ran a thumb across the crack.

“It lasted longer than I thought.”

The old man snorted.

“That’s because you refused to let it retire.”

“It still worked.”

“It was held together by hope.”

“It was a good sword.”

“It was a stick.”

“Same thing.”

Aldric shook his head.

Yet a small smile tugged at the corner of his mouth.

That evening, after dinner, the old man disappeared into the cabin.

When he returned, something rested in his hands.

Wrapped in cloth.

Rowan frowned.

“What’s that?”

“Open it.”

Carefully, he unwrapped the bundle.

Then froze.

A sword.

Not wood.

Not a practice blade.

Not a toy.

A real sword.

Simple.

Well-made.

Worn by time.

Yet cared for.

For several moments Rowan simply stared.

“A real one?”

“A fake one would be useless.”

The boy immediately drew it.

The blade flashed in the firelight.

Aldric promptly took it away.

“Rule one.”

“What?”

“Don’t stab yourself.”

“Why would I stab myself?”

The old man looked him directly in the eye.

“Because you’re you.”

Rowan couldn’t argue.

The evidence was overwhelming.

Life continued.

The first time Aldric sent him hunting alone, Rowan returned carrying a deer.

The old man pretended not to be impressed.

Failed.

The first time Rowan repaired part of the cabin roof himself, it collapsed three days later.

The second attempt lasted longer.

The third survived.

Progress.

One afternoon Rowan reached for a sack of grain.

Aldric reached for it too.

Both stopped.

The old man stared.

“What?” Rowan asked.

Aldric shook his head.

“Nothing.”

Years ago, Rowan had needed both hands to lift it.

Now he carried it over one shoulder.

The realization lingered with Aldric long after the moment passed.

One evening Rowan sat outside carving wood.

The mountain breeze drifted through the trees.

The knife moved carefully.

Shaping.

Refining.

Creating.

Eventually he finished.

A small wooden horse rested in his palm.

For a long moment he stared at it.

Then placed it on the windowsill inside the cabin.

Beside a few other small carvings collected over the years.

Aldric noticed.

Neither spoke.

Some things didn’t need words.

The mountain remained the same.

Hunts.

Training.

Repairs.

Meals.

Arguments.

Home.

Yet every now and then Rowan found himself looking toward the path leading down the mountain.

Not waiting.

At least that’s what he told himself.

One afternoon he climbed higher than usual.

Past familiar trails.

Past familiar cliffs.

To a place overlooking the valley below.

The world stretched endlessly before him.

Forests.

Roads.

Villages.

Distant mountains.

A world he had never seen.

A world that no longer felt impossibly far away.

For a long time he stood there.

Watching.

Wondering.

Then a voice drifted across the wind.

“You’re getting harder to find.”

Rowan froze.

Slowly, he turned.

A girl stood several paces away.

Older.

Taller.

Her hair longer than before.

Yet instantly familiar.

A worn travel bag rested on her shoulder.

And hanging from it…

A small wooden horse.

For a moment neither spoke.

The mountain wind drifted between them.

Rowan had imagined this moment countless times.

None of those versions had prepared him for the real thing.

Finally, he spoke.

“You got taller.”

The girl stared.

Then laughed.

A familiar laugh he hadn’t heard in two years.

“So did you.”

For the first time in two years…

Elara had come back.
