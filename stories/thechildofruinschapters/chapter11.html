The Child of Ruin

Chapter 11: The Farewell

🏠 StoryForge

The cabin felt strange that morning.

Not because anything was different.

But because everything was.

Elara’s bag sat beside the table.

Packed.

Ready.

Waiting.

Rowan stared at it.

Then looked away.

Then looked back.

As though if he watched it long enough, it might disappear.

Unfortunately, it remained exactly where it was.

“Do you have enough food?”

Elara didn’t even look up.

“Yes.”

A few minutes passed.

“Are you sure?”

“Yes.”

Another few minutes.

“What if you run out?”

The girl slowly lowered the shirt she was folding.

“Rowan.”

“What?”

“Stop.”

The boy looked genuinely confused.

“I’m helping.”

“No.”

“I am.”

“No.”

Rowan considered arguing.

Then wisely decided against it.

For almost thirty seconds.

“What if—”

“Rowan.”

“Right.”

The silence lasted another minute.

“Do you have enough water?”

Elara buried her face in her hands.

Outside, Aldric laughed.

Just once.

The boy immediately pointed.

“Grandpa laughed.”

“I did not.”

“You did.”

“I didn’t.”

“You smiled.”

“That wasn’t a smile.”

“It looked like one.”

The old man grumbled something under his breath and returned to chopping wood.

The morning passed slowly.

Too slowly.

Normally Rowan filled every quiet moment with questions.

Today he mostly followed Elara around.

Like a particularly stubborn shadow.

When she packed her bag.

He watched.

When she checked her supplies.

He watched.

When she filled her waterskin.

He watched.

Eventually she turned around.

“Why are you following me?”

Rowan blinked.

“I’m not.”

“You absolutely are.”

The boy looked behind him.

As if expecting to find somebody else.

“Oh.”

That somehow wasn’t reassuring.

Near midday, Elara stepped outside.

The mountain breeze brushed against her face.

The familiar cabin stood quietly behind her.

For a moment she simply looked at it.

Trying to remember every detail.

The door.

The chimney.

The clearing.

The stream beyond the trees.

Home.

Not her home.

Yet somehow it felt like one.

“Here.”

The voice came from behind her.

Elara turned.

Rowan stood there holding something.

A small wooden figure.

A horse.

It wasn’t perfect.

One leg was slightly shorter than the others.

The head leaned a little to one side.

And the carving marks were still visible.

But it was unmistakably a horse.

The girl stared.

“You made this?”

Rowan nodded.

“Last night.”

“You stayed up?”

“Maybe.”

That meant yes.

Elara carefully accepted it.

Turning the little horse over in her hands.

“It’s nice.”

The boy immediately smiled.

“Now you have a horse.”

“It’s made of wood.”

“Still counts.”

The confidence with which he said it made arguing impossible.

So she didn’t.

The afternoon eventually arrived.

Then, unfortunately, it ended.

The time had come.

Elara adjusted her bag.

Checked her supplies one last time.

Then faced the cabin.

Aldric stood beside the door.

Arms crossed.

Watching quietly.

Rowan stood beside him.

Trying very hard not to look disappointed.

Failing.

“Take care of yourself.”

Aldric said.

The girl nodded.

“I will.”

The old man hesitated.

Just briefly.

“You’re always welcome here.”

The words were simple.

Yet they meant more than he would ever admit.

Elara smiled.

“Thank you.”

Then she looked toward Rowan.

The boy shoved his hands into his pockets.

Suddenly finding the ground very interesting.

Several seconds passed.

Finally:

“Don’t get lost.”

The girl laughed.

A small laugh.

Soft and warm.

“You were the one who found me.”

“Still.”

The answer came immediately.

“Don’t get lost.”

For a moment neither spoke.

Then Elara nodded.

“I won’t.”

The promise felt important.

Even if neither knew why.

With one final look, she turned.

And began walking down the mountain path.

Step by step.

Slowly growing smaller.

The wind stirred the trees.

The mountain remained silent.

Halfway down the trail, Elara looked back.

Rowan was waving.

Both arms.

Like she might somehow miss him otherwise.

She laughed and waved back.

Beside him, Aldric stood motionless.

As always.

Then, just before she turned away again—

The old man raised a hand.

Only once.

Only briefly.

But she saw it.

And smiled.

Then she continued down the mountain.

Until the trees swallowed the path.

And she disappeared from sight.

The clearing felt strangely empty afterward.

Dinner was quiet.

Too quiet.

Nobody argued.

Nobody complained.

Nobody asked questions.

Even Rowan seemed to notice.

The empty chair across the table felt wrong.

The cabin felt larger.

Yet somehow emptier.

That night, long after the fire had burned low, Rowan looked toward the doorway.

Half expecting Elara to walk through it.

Complaining about being lost.

Or arguing about roads.

Or telling him his city looked terrible.

She didn’t.

The mountain had not changed.

The cabin had not changed.

Nothing had changed.

Yet somehow…

Everything felt different.
