The Child of Ruin

Chapter 10: The Promise

🏠 StoryForge

“Grandpa! Catch!”

A wooden sword spun through the air.

Aldric didn’t even look up.

The weapon sailed past him.

Past the chopping block.

Past the pile of firewood.

And disappeared into a nearby bush.

Silence.

The old man slowly lowered his axe.

“That was not catching.”

Rowan frowned.

“You missed.”

Aldric stared.

The boy stared back.

At some point over the years, Rowan had grown.

The child who once got trapped beneath a bucket now stood nearly as tall as Aldric’s shoulder.

His limbs had grown longer.

His voice deeper.

His wooden swords larger.

Yet somehow his talent for causing trouble had grown even faster.

“I didn’t miss.”

Aldric pointed toward the bush.

“You threw it halfway down the mountain.”

Rowan followed his gaze.

The sword’s handle poked out from the leaves.

“…It curved.”

“It is a stick.”

“It curved.”

The old man sighed.

There was simply no winning some arguments.

By afternoon, Rowan was making his way home alone.

A rabbit hung from his pack.

A pouch filled with herbs rested at his waist.

The familiar mountain trail stretched before him.

Every tree.

Every stream.

Every rock.

He knew them all.

This mountain was his world.

Which was why the footprints immediately caught his attention.

Rowan stopped.

They weren’t animal tracks.

They weren’t Aldric’s.

And they definitely weren’t his.

Someone else had been here.

Curiosity won almost immediately.

It usually did.

The tracks wound through the forest.

Past a stream.

Across a narrow trail.

And finally ended beneath a large tree.

A girl sat there.

She looked around his age.

Maybe a little older.

Her clothes were dusty.

Her hair was messy.

Her expression suggested she was having a terrible day.

For several moments neither spoke.

Then Rowan pointed.

“You’re lost.”

The girl frowned.

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

“I’m not.”

“Then why are you sitting out here?”

The girl crossed her arms.

“Because I want to.”

Rowan looked around.

At the trees.

The bushes.

The empty mountainside.

Then back at her.

“That’s weird.”

“You’re weird.”

The conversation somehow felt hostile despite lasting less than a minute.

A loud growl interrupted the silence.

Both looked downward.

The girl’s face immediately turned red.

“I didn’t hear anything.”

Rowan nodded.

“Your stomach did.”

The girl looked like she wanted to throw a rock at him.

Unfortunately, she appeared too hungry to try.

Without a word, Rowan sat down on a nearby rock.

The girl looked suspicious.

Then confused.

Then suspicious again.

“What are you doing?”

“Waiting.”

“For what?”

“For you to admit you’re hungry.”

“I’m not hungry.”

Her stomach betrayed her immediately.

The growl echoed through the trees.

Rowan smiled.

The girl looked away.

Defeated.

A few minutes later she was eating dried rabbit meat so quickly that Rowan became slightly concerned.

“When did you last eat?”

The girl paused.

“Yesterday.”

Rowan nearly choked.

“That’s stupid.”

The girl’s eyes narrowed.

“Says the boy talking to strangers in the middle of a forest.”

“You’re the stranger.”

“You’re weird.”

“You already said that.”

“I’m saying it again.”

Rowan wasn’t entirely sure what had just happened.

Somehow he felt like he had lost.

As the sun began to set, Rowan stood.

The girl looked up.

“Come on.”

She blinked.

“What?”

“Home.”

The answer came so naturally that she couldn’t immediately respond.

“Why?”

Rowan shrugged.

“Because you’re hungry.”

The girl stared.

The answer was so simple it destroyed every argument she had prepared.

The walk back to the cabin was surprisingly quiet.

Occasionally Rowan pointed things out.

A rabbit burrow.

A stream.

A tree he had fallen out of.

Twice.

The girl corrected him.

Three times.

Rowan insisted the third one didn’t count.

Eventually the cabin came into view.

A chimney.

Warm light shining through the windows.

The smell of food drifting through the air.

For the first time all day, the girl’s expression softened.

Just a little.

Rowan pushed open the door.

“Grandpa! I’m back!”

Aldric looked up from the table.

“You’re late.”

Then his eyes moved past Rowan.

Silence.

The old man’s gaze settled on the girl.

The girl suddenly felt like a rabbit being examined by a wolf.

A tired wolf.

But a wolf nonetheless.

Several seconds passed.

Then Aldric pointed toward the door.

“No.”

The door immediately closed.

Rowan stared at it.

Then knocked.

“Grandpa.”

“No.”

“Grandpa.”

“Absolutely not.”

The girl covered her mouth.

Trying very hard not to laugh.

For some reason, that made Rowan even more annoyed.

A minute later the door opened again.

Aldric rubbed his forehead.

The expression of a man who had already lost the argument.

“One night.”

Rowan grinned.

The girl stepped inside.

The old man immediately regretted everything.

Dinner was chaos.

Rowan asked questions nonstop.

Where was she from?

Why was she alone?

How did she get here?

Did she always sit in random forests?

The girl eventually threatened to throw a spoon at him.

Aldric considered allowing it.

For the first time in years, three people sat around the table.

The cabin felt different.

Louder.

Warmer.

Alive.

Even Aldric seemed to notice.

Though he would never admit it.

As the evening grew late, the conversation finally slowed.

The girl sat quietly near the fireplace.

The warm light danced across the room.

Her gaze drifted toward Rowan.

For the first time she properly noticed his eyes.

One gold.

One crimson.

The girl frowned.

Something about them felt strangely familiar.

A memory lingered just beyond her reach.

Yet no matter how hard she tried, she couldn’t grasp it.

The fire crackled softly.

Outside, night settled over the mountain.

Inside, Aldric folded his arms.

“Well.”

The girl looked up.

“What’s your name?”

For a moment she hesitated.

Then quietly answered.

“Elara.”

The cabin fell silent.

And without realizing it, all three of their lives had just begun to change.
